## What's changed since the original proposal

The site now includes a working **board portal** — a private admin area where
board members manage the site and the dues ledger themselves, with no
webmaster in the loop for day-to-day updates:

- **Announcements:** post once in the portal; it appears on the homepage and
  the community page archive immediately.
- **Shared inbox:** every form on the site (contact updates, suggestions,
  issue reports, exterior change requests) lands in one queue the whole board
  can see — no more requests lost in one volunteer's personal email.
- **Dues ledger:** all ~170 households in one list — import from a
  spreadsheet, mark payments as they arrive, export back to a spreadsheet
  anytime. Live totals for the treasurer.
- **Site content editing:** pool hours and season dates are edited in the
  portal and appear on the pool page — no more "the website says last
  year's hours."
- **Automatic weekly backups** and one-click export of all association data.
  The Association's data is never locked in.

You can try it today at **https://wopha-website.pages.dev/portal/** with
sample data. The demo is intentionally left open so the whole board can
click around without accounts; before any real resident data goes in, it
gets locked behind board-member email login (each of you receives a one-time
PIN by email — nothing to install, no passwords to manage).

## Three additions included in this scope

1. **Announcement emails, done right.** Posting an announcement in the
   portal also emails every resident individually — no more open-CC blasts
   like the one that exposed 200+ member addresses this May. The mailing
   list maintains itself from the contact-update form and the ledger.

2. **Automatic payment reconciliation.** When a household pays dues through
   the site, the ledger marks them paid on its own — the payment carries the
   property address, so nothing is hand-matched. The treasurer's job becomes
   reviewing a list, not reconstructing it from bank statements.

3. **Dues reminders and a collection dashboard.** Households that haven't
   paid get automatic, individually-addressed reminder emails as the
   deadline approaches (schedule set by the treasurer). The portal shows a
   live collection bar — how many paid, how many outstanding, dollars in.

## What it would cost (replaces Options A and B)

| | |
|---|---|
| One-time fee | **$1,300** |
| Annual fee | **$99/year** |

**The one-time fee covers:** everything in the original proposal's launch
fee (content migration, Stripe setup with the treasurer, forms and calendar
wiring, zero-downtime DNS cutover, directory lockdown, board email routing)
**plus** the board portal above and the three automations, delivered and
demonstrated.

**The annual fee covers:** hosting and domain management, the email-sending
service, security updates, board-login changes as officers turn over, and
technical support. It's this low because the portal replaces the monthly
webmaster retainer — the board updates its own content now.

**For comparison:** PayHOA charges $199/month at our community size —
$2,388 every year — for a portal like this. This proposal is $1,399 in year
one and $99/year after. Direct platform costs stay tiny (about $10–12/year
for the domain; hosting and the email tier are free at our size), and those
are billed to the Association directly — the $99 is for the human, not
markup.
